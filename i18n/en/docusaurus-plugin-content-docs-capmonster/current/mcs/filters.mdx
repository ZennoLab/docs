## Description.  
Graphic filters help prepare a CAPTCHA for training: bring the image to the required color, remove noise, and—most importantly—scale it correctly.  

### What should a CAPTCHA look like after processing?  
Ideally, after applying filters, your CAPTCHA should be:  
- **black and white** — no shades of gray (letters are black, background is white);  
- **free of noise** — without lines, dots, circles, or spots;  
- **medium-sized** — for simple CAPTCHAs, or enlarged by about 1.5–2× for complex ones.  
_______________________________________________
## Types of filters.  
### Two sets.
There are two types of filters:  
**1.** ***CAPTCHA filters*** — applied to the entire image.  
**2.** ***Character filters*** — used when processing already extracted characters.  

After filtering, the CAPTCHA and the characters should look the same, so the filter sets usually almost match.  

But there are exceptions:  
- the **edge cropping** filter is needed only for CAPTCHAs;  
- a filter that **removes part of the image** (for example, in numeric ReCaptcha—the background with the house number) is also applied only to the CAPTCHA, not to the characters.  

That’s why the filters are divided into two groups.  
_______________________________________________
### The most important filters.  
#### Threshold.  
*Makes the image completely black and white.*  

Each pixel becomes either black or white depending on the selected threshold value, which you can set in the filter settings.  

#### Resize.  
*Changes the image size.*  

Remember: increasing the scale makes characters larger, which means it increases the size of the character recognition window and the load on the core. As a result, CAPTCHA recognition time increases.  
_______________________________________________  
:::warning **We do not recommend overusing filters.**
In trying to completely remove all noise, you risk actually making the training results worse.
:::
